Subject: Verification agent: diagnosis, independent checks, the OpenPINN plate as a held-out test, and a correction

Dear Professor Rabczuk,

Thank you for your detailed reply and for the OpenPINN paper. I have worked through your points in the order of your priorities. The updated technical report is attached; Section 28 maps each of your points to what was done.

First, a correction. While building an independent 3D assembly for the mixed-formulation check, I found that the stored B3 stresses had been converted to Cauchy stress twice: the FE library already returns the Cauchy stress, and our post-processing applied the conversion again. Displacements, the reaction moment, the strain energy and the surrogate-error evaluation are not affected. I corrected the code, repeated every B3 solve, and recomputed the manuscript's groove-stress numbers; they change by a few percent (for example, the averaged sigma_xx range 0.38-0.44 becomes 0.42-0.47), and the conclusions do not change.

What was done:

1. Diagnosis of non-convergence. For every quantity without a demonstrated rate, the agent now assigns a cause from five classes using 15 hypotheses with tests on the stored data, runs the cheap missing tests itself and names the next investigation. It separates the B1 corner singularity from the B3 finite-radius concentration.
2. Independent checks. A second FE code reproduces the B1 and B2 solvers to 1e-12. On B3 I added two mixed formulations. With a linear dilatation per element (Q20/P1), the groove averages on uniform meshes agree with the displacement formulation within 1.6% at 15,960 and 1.4% at 23,520 elements, a gap that shrinks with refinement (the 23,520 level needed a new iterative solver); the earlier 3-8% disagreement came mainly from the constant-pressure F-bar.
3. Held-out benchmarks. On Cook's membrane, pre-registered, the unchanged rules predicted 4 of 5 verdicts. On the plate with a hole from the OpenPINN paper, also pre-registered, they predicted only 1 of 6, because the singular-order check exists only for linear elements. A prediction committed before the finest solve, that the stress maximum moves from the hole to a clamped corner, held in all five parts.
4. The OpenPINN reference. Its single FE mesh (h = 0.025) has an energy-norm error of about 1% by our estimate, adequate for the 3.5-6.3% displacement errors reported, but not for a peak stress, which has no finite limit at the corners.
5. Surrogate errors under the converged definition. With the Gaussian-weighted groove averages, which the agent certifies with an uncertainty of 0.11-0.18%, the best surrogate still errs by 146-173%. The groove peak itself remains unverified: on locally refined meshes every formulation I tried, including Q20/P1, fails.
6. LLM against rules. In nine billed runs the LLM recovered the problem specification from text every time and missed fewer B3 failures than the rules, but raised many false alarms that varied from run to run.
7. Manuscript. The groove numbers are corrected, and the text now limits the stability claim to region-averaged normal stresses.
8. CSM/CFM. On the open-hole lamina of the CFM paper, the agent's discretization uncertainty at the paper's mesh is far below the constitutive range (29-143 times below its half-width), so the combined bound is dominated by the constitutive part.
9. Next agents. A literature agent now turns a paper into a benchmark card in which every field must quote the paper, checked in code. On the OpenPINN plate and the CFM lamina it found the right section and every quote it gave was in the paper; it passed 7 of 8 checks on each, both misses coming from my card design (for example, it called the lamina's hole a concentration, but the code read every listed feature as a singularity). After fixing that it passed 8 of 8 on both. On a paper it had not seen (two sections of the CSM paper, with the expected cards committed beforehand) it then passed 6 of 6 and 8 of 8. An active learner that uses the verified error of each mesh level reaches a 1% surrogate error about 16 times cheaper than active learning on the fine mesh alone, and is the only strategy to reach 0.1%; without the verified errors it stalls at 1.1% (report Section 24).
10. Remaining items (report Section 25). An unseen L-shaped panel, pre-registered, confirmed the proposed singular-order extension for quadratic elements in 10 of 11 expectations. An independent spline discretization of the CFM lamina with exact geometry (an IGA-type 2D pilot) reproduces the FE values to 2e-6. A one-mesh adjoint error estimate works for smooth quantities but cannot see geometric error, and an AMG-preconditioned solver replaces the direct solver where memory runs out.
11. Human intervention. 59 interventions are counted and categorised; only 6 steps are fully automated.

Questions:

1. Singular-order check: I propose to adopt the extension to quadratic elements, which follows from standard corner theory, and to report an energy order above 2*lambda as "pre-asymptotic, a finer level is needed" rather than as a pass. Both changes came after seeing held-out results; the first unseen test (an L-shaped panel) confirmed the extension in 10 of 11 expectations, and showed one quantity approaching its rate from below, which the label does not cover. Is that acceptable?
2. Absolute requirements: following your point on CSM and CFM, I propose to set the numerical requirement of a quantity from its constitutive range (discretization uncertainty at most one third of the CSM/CFM half-width), with 1% for global quantities and displacements and 5% for region-averaged stresses as interim values where no such range exists. Is this the right direction?
3. Groove peak: higher-order and mixed formulations are done; FEM against IGA, which you asked for, is what remains. A 2D spline pilot on the lamina shows the gain of exact geometry, but the groove needs a 3D NURBS hyperelastic solver. Should that come next?
4. CSM and CFM: I have read both papers on arXiv. As a first step towards your goal of certified bounds, I computed the numerical half for the CFM open-hole lamina (report Section 23, pre-registered): at the paper's resolution the discretization error of the compliance and the strain averages is 29 to 143 times smaller than the half-width of the constitutive range, and an independent spline discretization reproduces the FE values to 2e-6. Shall we combine it with your ranges on the same mesh family, and could I have access to the code?
5. Geometry families and industrial material models (your earlier point for the surrogate study): which should come first, for example the groove depth and width of B3 as operator inputs, and which material models beyond neo-Hookean, Mooney-Rivlin and Arruda-Boyce? I have not started this without your choice of scope.

Best regards,
