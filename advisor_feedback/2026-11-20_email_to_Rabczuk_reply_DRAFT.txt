Subject: Verification agent: diagnosis, independent checks, the OpenPINN plate as a held-out test, and a correction

Dear Professor Rabczuk,

Thank you for your detailed reply and for the OpenPINN paper.

I have worked through your recommendations, prioritizing independent verification, previously unseen benchmarks, and comparison with conventional verification. The updated technical report is attached; Section 28 maps your comments to the completed work and remaining questions.

First, an important correction: While developing an independent 3D implementation, I discovered that the stored B3 stresses had undergone a double Cauchy-stress conversion. I corrected the post-processing, repeated the B3 calculations, and updated the affected manuscript results. The stress values changed by a few percent, and two of 41 verification verdicts changed, but the main conclusions remain unchanged. The manuscript now limits its stability claim to region-averaged normal stresses and clarifies that the estimated Gaussian-average uncertainty describes the Q20 refinement sequence, not necessarily the error relative to the continuum solution.

The main developments are:

1. Diagnosis of non-convergence: The agent now evaluates 15 diagnostic hypotheses across five cause categories, executes available low-cost tests, and identifies further investigations. It distinguishes the B1 corner singularity from the finite-radius stress concentration in B3.

2. Independent numerical checks: A second FE implementation reproduces the B1 and B2 solutions to approximately 10^-12. For B3, the Q20/P1 mixed formulation agrees with the displacement formulation within 1.6% at 15,960 elements and 1.4% at 23,520 elements. The earlier 3-8% discrepancy arose mainly from the constant-pressure F-bar formulation. Additional spline-based discretizations and manufactured-solution checks were performed, including in 3D.

3. Held-out benchmarks: The framework was tested on B2, Cook's membrane, the OpenPINN plate, the CFM lamina, an L-shaped panel, and an edge crack. The original rules revealed limitations for quadratic elements. A proposed singular-order extension was subsequently evaluated on the L-shaped panel and edge crack, confirming 10 of 11 and 11 of 12 pre-registered expectations, respectively.

4. OpenPINN reference: A comparable Q4 FE mesh at the reported resolution (h = 0.025) gives an estimated energy-norm error of approximately 1.07%. This suggests that the reference is adequate for the reported displacement errors of 3.5-6.3%, but not as a converged reference for pointwise peak stress at the singular corners. The paper's exact reference mesh was not available for direct verification.

5. B3 surrogate verification: The Gaussian-weighted groove averages show estimated uncertainties of 0.11-0.18% within the Q20 family. However, an independent spline discretization gives values approximately 2% higher. The best surrogate still has errors of 146-173%, so the conclusion about its inadequate local stress accuracy is unaffected.
To address this issue, the agent now requires agreement with an independent discretization before treating an uncertainty estimate as final. Of 34 rate-certified quantities, 21 pass this cross-check, five show disagreement, and eight still lack an independent reference.

6. Agent versus conventional verification: Among 68 quantities classified as verifiable, conventional three-level Richardson/GCI verification accepts 46, whereas the extended framework accepts 62. The singular-order extension was developed after some held-out results, so this improvement is not yet fully independently validated. Neither approach accepted the 14 non-verifiable peaks.
Importantly, neither the agent nor conventional GCI detected the two largest discrepancies in this project - the double stress conversion and the approximately 2% Gaussian-average difference. Both were identified through independent implementations.

7. CSM/CFM: For the CFM open-hole lamina, the estimated discretization uncertainty is 29-143 times smaller than the constitutive uncertainty half-width. An independent spline discretization reproduces the corresponding FE quantities to approximately 2 x 10^-6.

8. Literature and active-learning agents: A source-checked literature agent has been tested on previously unseen sections of the CSM paper. A preliminary verification-aware multi-fidelity active learner reaches a 1% surrogate error at approximately 16 times lower computational cost than its fine-mesh-only baseline.

9. Remaining limitations: The framework still requires substantial human involvement: 59 interventions were documented, of which only six are now fully automated. Although the solvers passed manufactured-solution verification cases, one groove-parametrised manufactured solution on the Q20 meshes remains unexplained. It fails for both formulations, suggesting an issue with the test field or setup rather than a formulation-specific solver defect.

I would appreciate your guidance on the following questions:

1. Would you support adopting the proposed quadratic-element singular-order extension, with an inconclusive verdict when the observed energy order exceeds 2*lambda and may still be pre-asymptotic? The current criterion does not cover cases approaching the theoretical order from below.
2. For absolute accuracy requirements, would it be appropriate to require discretization uncertainty below one third of the CSM/CFM constitutive uncertainty half-width, with provisional limits of 1% for global quantities and displacements and 5% for region-averaged stresses?
3. For the B3 groove peak, displacement-only splines have not converged, while projected-dilatation splines show the sequence 1.71, 1.06, and 1.00. This suggests that near-incompressibility is an important contributing factor, but the peak is not yet certified. Would you recommend running the next prepared spline refinement on the HPC cluster?
4. Could we proceed toward combining numerical error estimates with your CSM/CFM uncertainty bounds, and would access to the corresponding code be possible?
5. Regarding geometry families and industrial constitutive models, which parameters and material models would you recommend prioritizing for the next surrogate study?
