Your answers to my five questions are now implemented: the singular-order extension is adopted, and pre-asymptotic convergence from either direction is classified as inconclusive until independently verified; the one-third margin and the QoI-dependent limits are in place; and the further spline refinement of the groove peak goes only through an experiment that the agent justifies and predicts itself (Sections 6 and 8.3 of the PDF). That experiment has now run on the cluster: the agent's prediction held (the last change of the peak fell from 11.0% to 3.6%), but the peak is not yet monotone, so it stays not verified. On the ten earlier problems these rules change one verdict of 83, and no missed failure appears.

To your question which parts of the workflow are truly autonomous: for 2D problems on domains built from squares (linear elastic, and since 25 November compressible neo-Hookean), the steps from choosing the paper to diagnosis now run without a person. These are the literature search, the problem definition from the text, meshing, a manufactured-solution check generated on the problem's own domain, simulation, convergence analysis and diagnosis. After them, the agent chooses its next experiments itself. Before each one it freezes a hypothesis and a numerical prediction; afterwards it scores them, and the outcome changes its choice of the next experiment.

Still manual are:
- geometries with holes, curved or inclined edges;
- 3D problems and material models other than linear elasticity and compressible neo-Hookean;
- submitting cluster jobs;
- confirming any input the agent had to assume.

In a test pre-registered on seven sources it had not seen, the agent stopped on every problem it cannot represent, with the right reason. It ran the representable ones from the text to certified verdicts, at USD 1.19 in LLM cost. Three of four predictions held; the miss and two weaknesses the test exposed are reported in the PDF, with the fixes made afterwards.

Since then (Section 8 of the PDF), compressible neo-Hookean problems run in the same loop, and the agent chooses the papers itself from arXiv. On a hyperelastic L-shaped bracket it certified nothing at the stated load. It then tested its own hypothesis that the corner's finite strains limit the rate, and the test confirmed it. Its first literature search found no paper whose example it can represent, mostly because of inclined edges; Cook's membrane was named in 4 of the 12 papers it screened in detail.

Following your order, the next step is geometry families (first inclined edges, which would open Cook's membrane, then holes and curved edges). After that come the 3D solvers in the same loop, and CSM/CFM at the end.
