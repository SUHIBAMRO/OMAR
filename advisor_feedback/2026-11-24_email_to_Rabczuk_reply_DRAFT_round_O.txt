Your answers to my five questions are now implemented: the singular-order extension is adopted, and pre-asymptotic convergence from either direction is classified as inconclusive until independently verified; the one-third margin and the QoI-dependent limits are in place; and the further spline refinement of the groove peak goes only through an experiment that the agent justifies and predicts itself (Section 6 of the PDF; prepared as a cluster job, not yet run). On the ten earlier problems these rules change one verdict of 83, and no missed failure appears.

To your question which parts of the workflow are truly autonomous: for 2D linear-elastic problems on domains built from squares, the steps from problem definition to diagnosis now run without a person. These are the problem definition from a text, meshing, a manufactured-solution check generated on the problem's own domain, simulation, convergence analysis and diagnosis. After them, the agent chooses its next experiments itself. Before each one it freezes a hypothesis and a numerical prediction; afterwards it scores them, and the outcome changes its choice of the next experiment.

Still manual are:
- choosing the paper;
- geometries with holes or curved edges;
- 3D and hyperelastic problems, whose solvers are not yet part of the loop;
- submitting cluster jobs;
- confirming any input the agent had to assume.

In a test pre-registered on seven sources it had not seen, the agent stopped on every problem it cannot represent, with the right reason. It ran the representable ones from the text to certified verdicts, at USD 1.19 in LLM cost. Three of four predictions held; the miss and two weaknesses the test exposed are reported in the PDF, with the fixes made afterwards.

Following your order, the next step is geometry families (holes and curved edges). After that comes the existing hyperelastic and 3D solvers in the same loop, and CSM/CFM at the end.
