Dear Omar,

With GRAFT, you are certainly in the right direction. I also attach one of our papers, currently on arxiv. You might want to look into other similar agents related to neural and traditional solvers such as FermiLINK, ALL-FEM, etc. I think the key requirements on the agent are clear :) and your results are interesting. Concerning your questions:

1. Gaussian-weighted averages look promising but I would not just replace the original B3 quantity. Please keep the original result for comparison, introduce the Gaussian-weighted average as a separate QoI and recompute the surrogate errors for this definition. Furthermore, the key question the agent should figure out is why we did not obtain convergence. There should be some classification such as mathematical/physical reason, numerical discretization issue, solver/implementation, QoI/postprocessing or surrogate-related. It should look at the problem's regularity, geometry, constitutive behaviour and numerical formulation to generate hypotheses, select appropriate diagnostic tests and autonomously decide which investigation is needed next. Simply refining the mesh or changing the averaging procedure is insufficient. The agent should also distinguish mathematical singularities (such as B1), where pointwise peak stresses cannot converge to a finite value, from finite-radius stress concentrations (such as B3). It should investigate alternative discretizations and formulations, including FEM versus IGA, mixed formulations and possible locking. Furthermore, the agent should understand and continually learn the hierarchy of physical and numerical models, including their assumptions, applicability, limitations, accuracy and computational costs. It should distinguish numerical non-convergence from inadequate physical modelling, identify when a different model or scale is required and autonomously select the appropriate model, verification strategy and next scientific investigation. This knowledge should continuously improve through experience with previously solved problems.

2. An uncertainty margin of three is ok for an initial (ad hoc) criterion, but we also need absolute accuracy requirements for engineering-relevant QoIs. We should clearly distinguish between numerical convergence estimates and mathematical certification. Concerning the latter: We are currently working on exactly such a certification framework. We call it Constitutive Set Mechanics (CSM), which provides certified bounds for constitutive models (currently for the worst MTPE), and we are extending it to general engineering-relevant QoIs. Then, Constitutive Fiber Mechanics (CFM) asks the question of the next experiment. So CSM starts with the constitutive model while CFM starts with the data. Both papers are now on arXiv. Ultimately, the verification agent should connect numerical error control with CSM/CFM model and parameter uncertainty to obtain certified bounds on engineering-relevant QoIs.

3. And yes, specify "region-averaged normal stresses" and distinguish Gaussian-weighted averages from the original sharp-region averages. Please avoid general claims about stress accuracy when shear components, stress norms and maxima did not demonstrate reliable convergence.

4. Please prioritize independent verification and testing on previously unseen benchmarks, followed by comparing the agentic framework against conventional deterministic verification. We need to demonstrate what the AI agent actually contributes. Please also quantify how much human intervention is required, include independent solver checks and 3D MMS and ensure the agent can recognize when the available evidence is insufficient and autonomously decide what additional investigation is needed. Afterwards, we should develop the literature agent and active learning.

Best regards,
Timon
